# SolarStack India

## Executive Summary

India has solved rooftop solar adoption faster than it has solved rooftop solar **usage**. Millions of households are adding rooftop systems under PM Surya Ghar, yet solar-only homes still depend on the grid for evening consumption and receive no backup during outages. Solar exported to the grid during the day is credited at rates below retail tariff in most DISCOMs, and generation lost to inverter trips or grid curtailment is unrecoverable — both problems that storage directly solves.[^1]

SolarStack is a retrofit battery platform for existing rooftop solar homes. The product adds a 7 kWh LFP battery, a 3.5 kW bidirectional inverter, grid-compliant power factor correction, app-based monitoring, and one-visit professional installation, allowing households to convert surplus daytime solar into evening savings and backup power without replacing their panels. [^2]

The business works because India-specific pricing can be materially lower than imported alternatives like Anker SOLIX, while customer payback remains close to solar-only economics. A locally installed system targets ₹1.44 lakh–₹1.96 lakh for storage plus installation versus about ₹2.18 lakh for Anker's imported equivalent, while delivering local service, Indian grid compliance, and a pathway to subsidy-linked deployment. [^3]

The long-term upside is larger than home storage alone. India is also building a unified EV-charging layer through the proposed Unified Bharat e-Charge platform, while PM E-DRIVE is funding nationwide charging buildout, creating a natural expansion path from home solar storage into home EV charging, energy orchestration, and eventually grid services. [^4]

## The Problem

### Rooftop solar has a structural gap

A typical urban Indian household consuming 300–500 units per month usually installs a 3 kW to 5 kW rooftop solar system, and 1 kW of rooftop solar can generate roughly 1,400–1,450 units annually in India. A household consuming around 400 units monthly may still spend roughly ₹40,000–₹50,000 per year on electricity, which is why rooftop solar adoption has accelerated under current subsidy programs. [^5]

The problem is that solar-only systems do not solve evening demand. Without storage, households still buy a large share of their evening and night electricity from the grid at full retail tariffs, even when they exported or wasted daytime solar generation earlier in the day. [^5]

### Solar owners face operational pain after installation

Grid-tied inverters in India commonly trip because of voltage swings, frequency deviations, thermal stress, and installation errors, and industry observers report many systems can trip 10–30 times per month during peak summer conditions. Net-metering approvals also face delays, with reporting indicating average waits around 50–60 days in some cases, which weakens the economics of oversized or export-dependent systems. [^6]

Post-installation experience is also weak. Many households lack real-time monitoring, fault alerts, backup power during outages, and reliable after-sales service, which means the customer often buys solar as a savings product but experiences it as a partially unmanaged hardware asset. [^7]

### Current solar economics create a storage opening

Residential rooftop solar is now affordable enough to create a large installed base but incomplete enough to justify storage. NDTV's reported cost ranges show that a 3 kW system costs about ₹1.65 lakh–₹2.10 lakh before subsidy and about ₹87,000–₹1.32 lakh after the central subsidy, while a 5 kW system costs about ₹2.75 lakh–₹3.50 lakh before subsidy and about ₹1.97 lakh–₹2.72 lakh after subsidy. [^5]

That means India is creating millions of rooftop customers first and solving storage second. As of March 2026, government-linked reporting said more than 2.62 million rooftop solar systems had been deployed under PM Surya Ghar, while MNRE's April 2026 physical progress data showed 26.75 GW of cumulative grid-connected rooftop solar nationwide. [^8]

## The Solution

### Product concept

SolarStack is a retrofit, AC-coupled storage system for homes that already have rooftop solar. It is designed to work with existing installations without requiring panel replacement and without forcing a full inverter swap, which mirrors the customer need Anker is addressing but with India-specific compliance, pricing, and service built in. [^9]

### Core product requirements

- 7 kWh LFP battery as the entry configuration, expandable in 7 kWh steps up to 42 kWh for larger homes or EV-charging use cases. [^2]
- 3.5 kW bidirectional inverter sized for typical evening residential loads and competitive with the Anker reference class. [^10]
- AC-coupled retrofit architecture so the product can be added to existing rooftop solar rather than requiring a full redesign of the customer's system. [^1]
- Active power factor correction targeting PF greater than or equal to 0.95 to support CEA-style grid-interactive compliance and reduce interconnection risk. [^11]
- Wide voltage and frequency ride-through to reduce shutdowns caused by Indian grid instability, especially in summer. [^12]
- App-based monitoring for generation, battery state, import/export, fault alerts, bill savings, and installer diagnostics. [^13]
- Professional installation with a standardized breaker-box and commissioning process, since Indian installations are highly customized and breaker misconfiguration is a real failure mode. [^14]
- Digital sizing workflow that uses bills, roof geometry, and sanctioned load to reduce over- and under-dimensioning risk. [^15]

### Why the battery matters

The battery turns rooftop solar from a daytime generation asset into an all-day savings asset. It raises self-consumption, reduces evening grid purchases, provides backup during outages, and recovers value otherwise lost from inverter tripping or delayed net-metering activation. [^12]

For the customer, the pitch is simple: keep the rooftop system already installed, add storage in one visit, and convert more solar units into avoided high-tariff consumption. [^2]

Anker SOLIX is the right reference product because it validates consumer demand for retrofit solar storage, but it is not localized for India. Public reporting on the Solarbank Max AC describes a 7 kWh modular system with a 3.5 kW bidirectional inverter, expandable capacity, and pricing starting around €2,299, which converts to roughly ₹2.18 lakh before any India-specific duties, localization, or service costs. [^9]

### Consumer payback

| Scenario | Net Investment | Annual Saving | Payback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Solar only, 3 kW, post-subsidy | ₹87,000–₹1.10 lakh | ₹25,000–₹34,000 | 3.2–4.4 years |
| Solar + SolarStack, post-subsidy-style target | ₹1.20 lakh–₹1.50 lakh | ₹35,000–₹46,000 | 3.3–4.3 years |
| Solar + imported Anker-like setup | ₹3.0 lakh–₹3.3 lakh combined | ₹35,000–₹46,000 | 6.5–9.4 years |

This is the central economic insight of the company. Storage adds far more value than it adds payback penalty when the system is locally built, properly sized, and sold into homes already paying high evening tariffs. [^5]

## Market, Expansion, and Business Case

### Why now

India's policy environment is pushing both rooftop solar and energy-adjacent infrastructure at the same time. Public reporting cites a ₹22,000 crore budget allocation for PM Surya Ghar in 2026, while the scheme targets 10 million homes by March 2027, creating a large installed base of future retrofit-storage buyers. [^16]

Battery economics are also improving. Reporting on India's storage market points to falling battery costs and a growing solar battery market projected to expand from about USD 214.6 million in 2024 to about USD 778.9 million by 2033, with a 15.4% CAGR. [^17]

### Business model

- Hardware sale at roughly ₹1.65 lakh ASP for the storage system, with margin improving as battery and inverter sourcing localizes. [^3]
- Installation and commissioning revenue through a certified partner network, which also protects service quality and reduces field failures. [^18]
- SaaS subscription for monitoring, fault alerts, usage analytics, and savings visibility, which is important because lack of monitoring is one of the biggest pain points in the current market. [^13]
- Financing facilitation through banks and NBFCs, since upfront cost and subsidy timing remain adoption bottlenecks. [^18]
- Longer-term grid and energy-services revenue once storage fleets can be orchestrated through common digital rails. [^19]

### Expansion into charging

India's EV-charging buildout creates a logical adjacent market rather than a separate one. Reporting indicates the government is pushing a national Unified Bharat e-Charge platform to let users discover, access, and pay across different charging networks through one trusted interface, explicitly positioning it as a UPI-like experience for EV charging. [^4]

At the same time, PM E-DRIVE has earmarked ₹2,000 crore for public charging infrastructure and related deployment, and government-linked reporting says the ministry has already approved charging proposals across states and public-sector entities in an initial phase. That creates a natural phase-two product path for SolarStack: home solar plus battery first, then home EV charger integration, then energy orchestration between rooftop generation, home battery, EV charging, and eventually grid interaction. [^20]

### Why the company can win

SolarStack does not need to beat incumbents at generic solar hardware. It needs to win where the market is currently broken: retrofit storage, installation standardization, software visibility, Indian grid resilience, DISCOM workflow, and home-energy-to-EV convergence. [^21]

### Fundraising message

Solar solved generation first. SolarStack solves monetization, reliability, and controllability next. The company enters through retrofit rooftop storage, proves superior customer economics versus imported alternatives, and expands into home charging and distributed energy orchestration as India's charging and digital energy stack matures. [^19]
